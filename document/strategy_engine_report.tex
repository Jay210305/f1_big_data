\title{Prescriptive Tyre Compound Management and In-Race Stint Strategy Engine\\
\large An End-to-End Big Data \& Machine Learning Pipeline over Formula 1 Telemetry (2021--2025)}
\author{Big Data Project}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We present an end-to-end pipeline that converts 57\,GB of raw Formula~1
telemetry (five seasons, $\sim$367k JSON files, 325.5M high-frequency samples)
into a prescriptive pit-window and stint-strategy engine. The system runs
entirely on a single laptop (32\,GB RAM, RTX~5070~Ti) using a memory-bounded
streaming ETL into a ZSTD-compressed Parquet lakehouse (16.7\,GB), a Gold
feature store with fuel-corrected degradation kinetics, a Remaining Useful
Life (RUL) regressor and cliff classifier trained with asymmetric losses, an
unsupervised driving-style profiler (autoencoder + HDBSCAN), and a Monte Carlo
strategy optimizer with scenario context and debounced cliff alerts. On an
out-of-sample backtest over 313 real strategic decision points from the 2025
season, the optimizer's recommendation improves on the actual executed
strategy in 77.6\% of cases with a median paired gain of $+18.0$\,s.
A traceable MapReduce runtime and a MongoDB operational store independently
verify batch transformations against Polars on shared data.
\end{abstract}

\section{System Architecture}
\begin{verbatim}
raw JSON (57 GB) -> Bronze/Silver Parquet lakehouse (16.7 GB, ZSTD)
  -> Gold feature store (fuel-corrected pace, rolling windows, tyre energy)
  -> Model 1: RUL + cliff (XGBoost/LightGBM/LSTM, asymmetric loss)
  -> Model 2: driving-style profiler (AE + HDBSCAN)
  -> Model 3: Monte Carlo pit-window optimizer + in-race pit monitor
  -> Backtest vs real 2025 strategies
\end{verbatim}
All processing is partitioned hierarchically
(season $\rightarrow$ GP $\rightarrow$ session $\rightarrow$ driver
$\rightarrow$ stint) with one session per worker; no season is ever held in
memory. The full pipeline is reproducible via a single orchestrator
(\texttt{src.run\_all}) with skip-if-cached stages.

\section{Data \& Lakehouse}
The TracingInsights dataset (2021--2025) provides per-lap telemetry at
$\sim$10--20\,Hz (\texttt{speed, throttle, brake, gear, rpm, drs, acc\_x/y/z,
distance, DriverAhead}), lap times with compounds and stint/life metadata,
weather, corners, and race-control messages. The ETL
(\texttt{src.ingest.etl}) converts 585 sessions into Hive-partitioned Parquet:
325.5M telemetry rows, 317k per-lap feature rows, and race-event tables, in
$\sim$11 minutes with 8 workers. Edge cases handled: \texttt{"None"} string
nulls, misaligned telemetry channels (truncated to the shortest aligned
subset), mixed-type JSON columns.

\section{Feature Engineering (Gold Store)}
Per-lap features combine telemetry aggregates (speed/throttle/brake/DRS
statistics, G-load integrals, $\sum a_{\text{lat}}^2 v$ tyre energy,
dirty-air fraction within 10\,m) with \emph{fuel-corrected} pace
($t_{\text{lap}} + 0.03\,\text{lap}$, races/sprints), causal rolling windows
($w\in\{3,5,8\}$ means, stds, medians, spreads, 5-lap slopes,
EMA$_{\alpha\in\{0.2,0.5\}}$), sector deltas (traction-limited S3 vs S1), and a
causal running-best gap (\texttt{pace\_above\_best}). Environmental context
(track/ambient temperature, rain, wind) and a rain wash-out track-evolution
index (chronological weekend rubber accumulation with $\gamma_{\text{wash}}=0.3$
wet-session resets) complete the store.

\section{Big Data Architectures: MapReduce and MongoDB}
The local multi-process runtime makes split, map, optional combine,
shuffle/sort, reduce and atomic commit explicit. Three real jobs aggregate
telemetry, fit stint slopes and describe driver/session profiles. Stable
CRC32 partitioning co-locates keys. An injected failure in map 3 produces
one retry and byte-identical output in all four final partitions.
This is a single-host prototype; multi-node scaling is not measured.

The shared labeled artifact has 107,606 rows and 85 columns. The full
stint trace verifies 106,735 emissions across 4,981 keys and four nonempty
partitions, preserving key/value multisets and independently reducing them.
Filtering explains the difference between map inputs and emissions.
MR and Polars agree within $3.56\times10^{-15}$ over 4,628 eligible stints
(4,274 dry, 354 wet). The saved model agrees for dry compounds only;
it has no wet entries, so its global match flag is false.

MongoDB stores embedded race sessions, labeled laps, Gold stints and

\section{Model 1: RUL \& Cliff Prediction}
\textbf{Labels.} Stints truncated by Safety Cars, DNFs, or short experimental
runs are filtered. The physical cliff $T_{\text{end}}$ is the first sustained
(3 consecutive laps) crossing of fuel-corrected rolling-median pace above the
stint best $+1.5$\,s; the target is piecewise-clipped
($\text{RUL}\le 15/22/30$ laps for S/M/H). All cliff-derived features are
excluded from the input matrix (leakage audit: none present).

\section{Model 2: Driving-Style Profiler}
In/out-laps, non-hot laps ($>107\%$ of session best) and SC/VSC laps are
excluded (44\% of laps removed). Each remaining lap is encoded as a
micro-sector vector (10 standardised distance bins $\times$
\{max lateral G, throttle, braking, speed\}) plus track-evolution and
environmental covariates. A GPU autoencoder (55$\to$16 latent) yields
driver-season signatures; HDBSCAN finds two archetypes among 172
driver-seasons (silhouette 0.76):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cluster 1} (115 driver-seasons; the established front-runners):
highest cornering load (lat-G 36.8) yet the gentlest fuel-corrected
degradation ($-0.040$\,s/lap) --- elite cornering efficiency.
\item \textbf{Cluster 0} (23; mostly rookies/test drivers): smoothest inputs
but steepest degradation ($-0.195$\,s/lap).
\item \textbf{Noise} (34): part-time and test drivers, no consistent archetype.
\end{itemize}

\section{Model 3: Pit-Window Optimizer}
A degradation model fitted on 2021--24 races (warm-up-excluded stint slopes:
$\beta_{\text{S}}=0.125 > \beta_{\text{M}}=0.092 > \beta_{\text{H}}=0.052$\,s/lap;
robust IQR $\sigma$; empirical cliff-lap distributions; pairwise within-driver
compound pace offsets) drives a Monte Carlo evaluator with analytic stint
times. Candidate 0/1/2-stop strategies are ranked per scenario (clean air /
DRS train $\beta\times1.323$ / backmarker $\beta\times1.891$) with p5--p95
confidence bands. An in-race monitor applies debounced cliff alerts
($\ge$2 consecutive laps above threshold) and a joint rule: a cliff alert with
RUL $>15$ laps is classified as transient thermal degradation, not wear.

2021--24 only):

The saved summary covers all 313 evaluated decisions across 21 races
(85 skipped), and the writer now retains the full history, so MongoDB imports
the same 313 documents as the summary. Matched stop counts: 223 decisions,
80.3\% wins, median +11.8\,s; divergent counts: 90 decisions, 70.0\%
wins, median +21.0\,s.

The backtest excludes the three rain-affected races by default: the wet
degradation and pace parameters are priors rather than fitted values, so
scoring them would measure the prior rather than the model. The
\texttt{--include-wet} flag opts in.

A realized-pace cross-check (\texttt{backtest\_realized.py}, 327 decisions)
recommends the same compound as the executed strategy in 47.1\% of the
decisions. Its delta is algebraically a restatement of the model's own
compound pace offsets, so it is a consistency check rather than an
independent validation; the independent check is the simulator calibration
described below.

\section{Limitations}
(1) The recommender and evaluator share the simulator (selection bias); true
counterfactual race outcomes are unknowable. (2) The optimizer leans one-stop
relative to actual field behaviour. (3) Pit loss is circuit-constant (21\,s).
(4) The scenario multipliers are fitted from the observed dirty-air
distribution, but that distribution is sparse, so they are floor-capped
estimates rather than extrapolations. (5) The wet compounds remain priors and
the rain-affected races are excluded from the backtest by default.
(6) Physical-target RUL is inherently noisy ($R^2\approx0.37$ ceiling after
leakage removal) --- the earlier $0.61$ was strategy-confounded. (7) The
simulator has no race-position term: the calibration residual is $+0.6$\,s
on average per stint but is larger for early stints and negative for late
ones, because neither track temperature nor traffic is modelled.

\section{Simulator calibration}
Scoring the optimizer with the same simulator that recommends the strategy
makes a systematic mis-calibration invisible to the backtest.
\texttt{src/models/calibrate.py} closes that loop by scoring the production
\texttt{optimizer.simulate()} against the strategies that were actually
executed in 2025 (387 dry stints). It found two structural errors: the
previous tyre's degradation was charged twice (once inside the inherited
base pace and again by the degradation term), and the pit-ENTRY lap --- the
lap the decision is normally taken on, 5--20\,s slower than the flying
pace --- was used as the base pace outside the backtest. With a fresh-pace
reference and a flying-lap base, the mean per-stint residual falls from
$+38.4$\,s to $+0.6$\,s and the median from $+39.7$\,s to $-1.7$\,s.
The backtest median paired gain rises from $+13.8$ to $+18.0$\,s and the
systematic under-stopping (1.51 recommended stops against 1.66 actually
run) disappears (1.73). The single underlying cause is the fixed
0.03\,s/lap fuel correction, which under-credits the pace the car
recovers as the fuel burns off; the residual coefficient that would absorb
it was measured ($-0.04$\,s/lap) but not adopted, because it shifts every
candidate equally and therefore cancels in the ranking.

\section{Conclusion}
A complete, reproducible, laptop-scale prescriptive strategy engine: from raw
JSON to ranked, uncertainty-quantified pit recommendations that beat real
2025 strategies in $\sim$77\% of decision points under the fitted
degradation physics. All code, metrics, and phase annotations are versioned;
the runbook (\texttt{RUNBOOK.md}) executes the full pipeline end-to-end.
